Demystifying Digital Misinformation

TruthLens (ancient Greek: Aletheia — uncovering truth) was conceived as an open natural language processing experiment to explore whether stylistic and syntactic signals can reliably assist humans in navigating online misinformation.

In the era of viral algorithms, synthetic generation, and rapid information cascades, determining source credibility is challenging. While manual fact-checking organizations perform essential forensic journalism, the volume of digital text requires complementary computational tools.

TruthLens investigates how specific linguistic signatures—such as emotional punctuation, sensationalized adjectives, unverified attributions, and scientific hedging—correlate with verified and debunked news reporting.

Hybrid Model Philosophy

Rather than relying solely on black-box neural networks, we combine interpretable Support Vector Machines (trained on stacked stylometry features) with fine-tuned RoBERTa transformer embeddings and explicit rule-based overrides.

shield

Privacy & Security First

TruthLens does not log or persist analyzed article text in database storage. Server logs strip text previews, CSRF protection secures web submissions, and memory-bounded rate limiters prevent abusive scanning.

Ethical Disclosures & Limitations

Probabilistic, Not Definitive: TruthLens produces statistical probabilities reflecting learned stylistic patterns. High model confidence indicates alignment with training characteristics—it does not constitute certified proof of factual reality.

Context Limitations: Satirical publications (e.g. The Onion), sarcasm, regional idioms, and emergent breaking developments may trigger unexpected classifications.

Human-in-the-Loop: TruthLens is designed as an investigative compass, not an automated judge. Always verify high-stakes claims through primary documentation and reputable journalistic wire services.
